Sprint Planning from purpose to practice
After this note you can
  • State the purpose, participants and timebox of Sprint Planning.
  • Explain the three topics (why, what, how) and who leads each one.
  • Say what the Scrum Guide does not prescribe, such as story points or velocity.

1Purpose, participants and timebox

ItemWhat the Guide says
PurposeSprint Planning initiates the Sprint by laying out the work to be performed for the Sprint.
Who creates the plan“This resulting plan is created by the collaborative work of the entire Scrum Team.”
PreparationThe Product Owner ensures that attendees are prepared to discuss the most important Product Backlog items and how they map to the Product Goal.
OthersThe Scrum Team may invite other people to attend to provide advice.
TimeboxA maximum of eight hours for a one-month Sprint. For shorter Sprints the event is usually shorter.
OutputThe Sprint Goal, the Product Backlog items selected and the plan for delivering them, together called the Sprint Backlog.
Exam angle

Sprint Planning is for the whole Scrum Team: Product Owner, Developers and Scrum Master. The plan is not handed down by the Product Owner, the Scrum Master or a manager, and the Developers are not told how to do the work.

2The three topics

Topic oneWhy is this Sprint valuable?The Product Owner proposes the value; the whole Scrum Team defines the Sprint Goal.
Topic twoWhat can be Done this Sprint?The Developers select items, in discussion with the Product Owner.
Topic threeHow will the work get done?The Developers plan the work, often in pieces of one day or less.
  • The Sprint Goal “must be finalized prior to the end of Sprint Planning.”
  • The more the Developers know about their past performance, their upcoming capacity and their Definition of Done, the more confident they are in their forecasts.
  • How the work is turned into an Increment is “at the sole discretion of the Developers. No one else tells them how.”
Old wording

The 2017 Guide had two topics (what, how) and called the Sprint Backlog a forecast by the Development Team. The 2020 Guide adds Topic One (why) and calls it a plan by and for the Developers. Exam questions may still use the old terms.

3What the Guide does not prescribe

Not in the GuideWhat to answer
A technique for sizing or forecasting (story points, hours, velocity)The Developers choose how they forecast. Relative story points are absent from the Guide.
A format for Product Backlog items (user stories)Attributes “often vary with the domain of work”.
Approval of the Sprint Backlog by a manager or the Scrum MasterThe plan belongs to the Developers.
A required number of items or hoursThe Developers decide how much they can do.

4Exam Q&A

S1. The Product Owner says that the Sprint needs no Sprint Goal because the Product Backlog items are clear. What should the Scrum Master advise?
  • AAgree: the Sprint Goal is optional when the items are clear and ordered, because they already show what the Sprint is for
  • BThe Scrum Team should define a Sprint Goal that explains why the Sprint is valuable and finalize it before Planning ends
  • CLet the Developers write the Sprint Goal after the Sprint starts, once they know how the work is going
  • DAsk the stakeholders to provide the Sprint Goal after the Sprint Review, when they have seen the Increment

Answer: B. Topic One of Sprint Planning is “Why is this Sprint valuable?”, and the whole Scrum Team defines the Sprint Goal, which must be finalized before the end of Sprint Planning. It is part of the Sprint Backlog and gives the Sprint coherence and focus.
